# 0004: Demo recording verification

## Context
The README demo is a GIF of `percolate render` replaying the recorded `small`
fixture. PR #37 generated it with ffmpeg and asked CI to fail when the committed
bytes drifted. That check fails on a different ffmpeg build or font rasterizer
even when every rendered frame is identical, so it proves the toolchain, not the
recording.

## Options
- Regenerate the GIF in CI's environment and commit those bytes: hides the
  problem until the next toolchain change, and needs CI access to resolve.
- Pin an ffmpeg binary and font in the repository: large binary artifacts with
  their own licensing and upgrade burden for a documentation image.
- Record the exact text frames and verify that the GIF replays them; let the
  encoder choose its own bytes.

## Decision
`demo/generate.sh` writes `demo/frames.sha256`: one SHA-256 per 80×24 plain
frame it feeds to ffmpeg. `make check` recomputes those frames from the fixture
through `internal/scene` and compares the hashes. It also decodes the GIF and
checks its size, frame count, looping, and that two frames look the same exactly
when their recorded text is the same, independent of palette and dithering. CI
runs `make demo`, repeats those checks on the freshly encoded GIF, and checks
the generator, tape, and manifest for drift. The GIF bytes themselves are not
compared.

## Consequences
A renderer change or a recording that no longer follows the fixture fails CI on
any ffmpeg build; a new encoder that draws the same frames does not. The GIF in
the repository remains an artifact generated by `make demo`, not a golden file.
No terminal, network, clock, font, or encoder enters the scene or its frame
checks.
